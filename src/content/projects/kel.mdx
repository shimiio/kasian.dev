## the pain

Working on projects like [pare](/projects/pare/), I always had five terminal tabs open, and every session I had to reopen them and `cd` into the right folders all over again. Two projects in parallel was worse: switching meant setting up another terminal from scratch, and sometimes that alone was enough to kill the urge to switch at all.

## the spark

Instead of hunting for a ready-made terminal, I got hooked on the idea of building my own. I picked Rust because I'd never touched it, and my C++ background felt like a decent starting point.

## the build

At first I went solo: I spent a long time learning how a PTY works, how a terminal thinks in cells and grids, and how rendering fits in. After about a month of on-and-off work, it hit me how big the job really was, and I got scared I'd spend forever on it with a result that mattered only to me.

So I brought in Claude Code and we wrote the terminal together. In the beginning I tried to understand every line; the further we got, the harder that became, and eventually I handed the code over to the agent almost entirely.

## now

I use kel almost every day, for projects and as a plain terminal. Each project lives in it as a saved set of tabs, so I don't lose context: whether it's [pare](/projects/pare/) or [discordLLM](/projects/discord-llm/), I can jump between them, or start something new, in seconds.
