## five tabs, every single time

**Every session started the same way: reopen five terminal tabs and `cd` into the right folders.** That's how working on [pare](/projects/pare/) went.

Two projects in parallel was worse. Switching meant setting up another terminal from scratch, and sometimes that alone killed the urge to switch at all.

## why not build my own?

**Instead of hunting for a ready-made terminal, I got hooked on the idea of building one.** I picked Rust because I'd never touched it, and my C++ background felt like a decent start.

## a month alone, then Claude Code

**Solo, I learned how a PTY works, how a terminal thinks in cells and grids, and how rendering fits in.** After about a month of on-and-off work it hit me how big the job really was. I got scared I'd spend forever on something that mattered only to me.

**So I brought in Claude Code, and we wrote the terminal together.** At first I tried to understand every line. The further we got, the harder that became, and in the end I handed the code over to the agent almost entirely.

## every project one keypress away

**I use kel almost every day, for projects and as a plain terminal.** Each project lives in it as a saved set of tabs, so I don't lose context.

[pare](/projects/pare/), [discordLLM](/projects/discord-llm/), or something new: I jump between them in seconds.
